Surface mode: fill the frame with a plain matte surface. White copier paper is about 0.85 reflectance, an 18% grey card is 0.18. The reading is the illuminance falling on that surface.

iPhone: photo from library
  1. Once: Settings → Camera → Formats → Most Compatible.
  2. In the Camera app, fill the frame with the surface you are measuring (or hold a sheet of paper flat over the lens for incident mode). Flash off. Shoot.
  3. Press the button and pick that photo.

The page reads the exposure record stored in the photo (shutter, ISO, aperture, metered brightness) and converts it to lux. Photos taken through the browser itself lose that record on iPhone, which is why the Camera app is used. Works on Android too. Each photo replaces the reading above; press Log reading to keep it.

Android: live camera
  1. Start the camera and allow access.
  2. Fill the dashed box with the surface you are measuring (or hold a sheet of paper flat over the lens for incident mode).
  3. Wait for the number to settle, press Hold 2 s, then Log reading.

Direct reading from the phone's own light sensor (the one that dims the screen). Usually within 10 to 20 percent and already cosine corrected. Point the screen at the light.

Calibration and lens settings

The app ships with a preset surface-mode factor measured on an iPhone against a reference meter, so it works without a meter. If you have one: put the phone and the meter side by side under the same light, enter what the meter says and press Calibrate. Your factor then replaces the preset on this device. Reset returns to the preset.

Aperture is used only when a photo carries no f-number. Reflectance applies in surface mode, transmittance in incident mode.

How this works and what to trust

The camera is used as a photometer. Each frame, the app reads the exposure the phone chose (shutter time and ISO) and the average linear brightness of the centre box, then solves the exposure equation for scene luminance: L = 12.5 × N² ÷ (ISO × t) × (Y ÷ 0.18) cd/m². In surface mode illuminance is E = π L ÷ reflectance. In incident mode a sheet of paper over the lens acts as a diffuser and E = π L ÷ transmittance.

Shutter and ISO come from the browser's camera settings. Android Chrome exposes them on most phones. iOS Safari does not, so on an iPhone this page can only show relative brightness, which is not a lux value. For iPhone an installed app that reads the camera's exposure data is needed.

Expected accuracy after one calibration against a real meter: roughly ±15 to 25 percent for lighting that resembles the calibration source. Camera sensors are not matched to the eye's V(λ) curve, so colour temperature and LED spectra shift the result. Without calibration, treat readings as ±50 percent. For compliance or handover documentation use a Class B or better lux meter.

The dashed box must be filled with an even, matte area. Avoid specular highlights, the light source itself, and strong gradients. The "clipping" badge appears when pixels saturate or the box is nearly black, which makes the reading unreliable.
